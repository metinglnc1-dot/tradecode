This strategy makes money. So why do so many of its paths fall into a hole?

I asked Claude to simulate it 10,000 times. Illustrative rules: 40% wins at +2R, losses at -1R. That is +0.2R per trade on average.

In 100 trades, the chance of at least one losing streak of:
5 in a row: about 98%
8 in a row: about 49%
10 in a row: about 20%

8 losses in a row, by win rate: 30% about 86%, 40% about 49%, 50% about 17%, 60% about 4%.

What 8 losses in a row do to the account:
1% risk per trade: 0.99⁸ = 0.92, about -8%
2% risk: 0.98⁸ = 0.85, about -15%
5% risk: 0.95⁸ = 0.66, about -34%
10% risk: 0.9⁸ = 0.43, about -57%

Try it with your own numbers: "My win rate is [X] percent, my average win is [Y]R and my average loss is 1R. Run a Monte Carlo of 100 trades and tell me the chance of 5, 8 and 10 losses in a row." 

Size your risk for the streak, not for the win rate.
